The story in brief

Two metrics lead everything else. Repeat-visit rate — the share of new members who return for a second visit — is the earliest signal in the data. It starts sliding six to seven months before churn and has fallen 20% or more at 74% of brands three months out. New signups follow a month or two behind, flagging about four in ten brands by M-3. Everything else — active users, redemptions, signup-to-first-check-in, active locations — moves only in the final one to two months, after the decision has usually been made.

Segments behave differently. Casual-dining gives the longest warning: 57% of these brands showed a falling repeat-visit rate six months out, and it is the only segment where signups collapsed as hard as repeat visits — a sign that servers stopped offering the program at the table. Quick-serve is quiet until four months out, then three-quarters of brands flag at once; high transaction volume masks decline. Brands under five years in loyalty show trouble a little earlier than mature brands, which hold volume until the last month or two and then drop off a cliff. Pizza is the largest churn cuisine at 17% of brands, mostly after five-plus years. Six of the seven c-stores left within five years — five of them in 2025 — with the strongest engagement in the dataset, a segment decision the loyalty metrics could not see.

If you want six months of warning, watch whether new members come back a second time. If you wait for sales, you get one.

How early the warning appears

All brands

% of brands with metric more than 20% below baseline, by month before churn

Insights
  • Repeat-visit rate moves first: a quarter of brands flagged by M-6, 74% by M-3, 95% by M-1.
  • New signups follow a month or two behind (38% by M-3).
  • Redemptions and active users move together in the last 1–2 months — lagging indicators. The drop reflects fewer active members, not weaker offers.
  • Signup-to-first-check-in (11% at M-3) and active locations (14%) barely move — the footprint stayed on the platform until the end, and the decline happened inside it.
Finding · age in loyalty

Younger brands show trouble earlier and more widely; mature brands hold on, then drop off a cliff. By M-4, 57% of under-5-year brands were 20%+ down on repeat-visit rate vs 49% of mature brands, and 38% vs 31% on new signups. Mature brands keep active users and locations steady until the final two months — a renewal not taken, not a slow fade. Baseline health was identical (retention 16.8% vs 16.3%); tenure changes the shape of the exit, not the strength of the program.

Under 5 years

Brands that churned within 5 years (37 brands)

Insights
  • Repeat-visit rate starts sliding at M-6 (24%) and reaches 57% by M-4 and 70% by M-3.
  • New signups is a stronger early flag here than for mature brands — 30% flagged by M-7, 43% by M-3.
  • Four of the six slow-fade brands sit in this group: programs that never fully embedded.
  • Median warning on repeat visits is 4 months versus 3 for mature brands — though with 76 brands that gap is not statistically significant.
Over 5 years

Brands that churned after 5+ years (39 brands)

Insights
  • Mature brands hold active users and locations until M-2 and then drop sharply — a renewal-not-taken pattern, not a slow fade.
  • Repeat-visit rate still flags 77% of brands by M-3, so it works regardless of tenure.
  • New signups stay flatter for longer (33% at M-3) — acquisition was not the problem for long-tenured brands.
  • Baseline health did not differ by age — tenure bought time, not engagement.
Finding · restaurant type

Casual-dining fails at the table; quick-serve fails quietly. Casual-dining gives the longest warning and is the only type where both metrics move together — by M-4, 86% were down on repeat visits and 50% on signups, a sign that servers had stopped offering the program. Casual-fast follows the standard slide (54% on repeat visits by M-4) while continuing to enrol members it cannot convert. Quick-serve barely moves until M-4 (38%) then flags almost all at once — high volume masks decline, so QSR gives about a quarter of notice. C-store shows no engagement signal on either metric; those exits were commercial.

Caveats
  • Churned brands with a loyalty user base above 5K are considered.
  • A metric is flagged when it falls 20% or more below the brand's own baseline. Normal month-to-month noise is within ±10%, so 20% is unlikely to be a single bad month, A higher cutoff such as 50% would be near-certain but only fires in the final one or two months — too late to act on.
  • C-store (7) and casual-dining (14) samples are small — read those segments as directional.
  • Without a retained-brand comparison, these charts show what churners had in common, not what distinguishes them from healthy brands.
