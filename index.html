Insights
  • Casual-fast is half of all churn (57 of 115) and skews mature — 31 of 57 left after five-plus years.

Cuisine × age

Split by cuisine type and years in loyalty

Insights
  • Pizza is the largest churn cuisine at 22 brands (19%) and skews mature — 13 of 22 left after five-plus years.
  • American (10) and Convenience Store (10) are the next largest and both overwhelmingly early churn — 8 and 9 under five years respectively.
  • Sandwich and Barbeque are entirely late churn (5 of 5 and 3 of 3 over five years); Asian leans late (5 of 8).
  • Delivery-heavy pizza brands struggle to hold a first-party loyalty program once third-party apps take over ordering.
Churn by year

Brands churning each year, by restaurant type

Insights
  • Churn has climbed every year: 5, 14, 16, 25, 29 — and 26 already in 2026 through July, on pace to be the highest year yet.
  • Casual-fast has been the largest segment every year since 2022 and accounts for 11 of the 26 churns in 2026 so far.
  • Quick-serve churn has grown from 6 brands across 2021–23 to 17 since 2024, and these are the largest footprints in the book.
  • C-store churn is a 2025–26 phenomenon — 8 of 10 brands in the last two years.

The story in brief

Two metrics lead. Repeat-visit rate — the share of new members who come back for a second visit — is the earliest signal: 20%+ down at half of churned brands four months out, versus 1% of healthy brands. New signups follow a month or two behind. Active users, redemptions and locations only move in the final one to two months.

Segments differ. Casual-dining gives the longest warning and is the only type where signups fall as hard as repeat visits — servers stopped offering the program. Quick-serve stays quiet until four months out, then flags all at once. Younger brands show trouble earlier and more widely; mature brands hold volume, then drop off a cliff at renewal. Pizza is the largest churn cuisine (17%), mostly after five-plus years. C-store exits were commercial — the loyalty metrics never moved.

Watch whether new members come back a second time. If you wait for sales, you get one month.

How early the warning appears

How to read these charts

Every brand is lined up on its own churn date, not the calendar. M0 is the month a brand churned; M-1 is the month before, M-2 two months before, back to M-12. This lets brands that churned in 2022 and 2026 sit on the same axis, so we can ask "what did the last year look like" for all 76 at once.

Each brand is compared with itself. Its M-12 to M-10 average is the baseline (100%). A metric is "showing trouble" when it falls 20% or more below that baseline. The lines show what share of the 76 brands were showing trouble in each month — the earlier a line rises, the more warning that metric gives.

All brands

% of brands with metric more than 20% below baseline, by month before churn

Insights
  • Effective — Repeat-visit rate. Moves first and furthest. Half of churned brands were 20%+ down four months before churn; only 1% of healthy brands ever show that. This is the metric to watch.
  • Effective, but later — New signups. A third of brands flagged by M-4, rising to 7 in 10 by M-1. Healthy brands rarely drop this far, so a fall is meaningful, but it arrives a month or two after repeat visits.
  • Not effective — Active users and redemptions. Barely move until the last two months. They confirm churn; they don't predict it.
  • Not effective — Active locations and signup-to-first check-in. Flat almost to the end. Stores stayed live and enrolment kept working; the program lost members, not footprint.
Finding · age in loyalty

Younger brands show trouble earlier and more widely; mature brands hold on, then drop off a cliff. By M-4, 57% of under-5-year brands were 20%+ down on repeat-visit rate vs 49% of mature brands, and 38% vs 31% on new signups. Mature brands keep active users and locations steady until the final two months — a renewal not taken, not a slow fade. Baseline health was identical (retention 16.8% vs 16.3%); tenure changes the shape of the exit, not the strength of the program.

Under 5 years

Brands that churned within 5 years (37 brands)

Insights
  • Repeat-visit rate starts sliding at M-6 (24%) and reaches 57% by M-4 and 70% by M-3.
  • New signups is a stronger early flag here than for mature brands — 30% flagged by M-7, 43% by M-3.
  • Four of the six slow-fade brands sit in this group: programs that never fully embedded.
  • Median warning on repeat visits is 4 months versus 3 for mature brands — though with 76 brands that gap is not statistically significant.
Over 5 years

Brands that churned after 5+ years (39 brands)

Insights
  • Mature brands hold active users and locations until M-2 and then drop sharply — a renewal-not-taken pattern, not a slow fade.
  • Repeat-visit rate still flags 77% of brands by M-3, so it works regardless of tenure.
  • New signups stay flatter for longer (33% at M-3) — acquisition was not the problem for long-tenured brands.
  • Baseline health did not differ by age — tenure bought time, not engagement.
Finding · restaurant type

Casual-dining fails at the table; quick-serve fails quietly. Casual-dining gives the longest warning and is the only type where both metrics move together — by M-4, 86% were down on repeat visits and 50% on signups, a sign that servers had stopped offering the program. Casual-fast follows the standard slide (54% on repeat visits by M-4) while continuing to enrol members it cannot convert. Quick-serve barely moves until M-4 (38%) then flags almost all at once — high volume masks decline, so QSR gives about a quarter of notice. C-store shows no engagement signal on either metric; those exits were commercial.

Caveats for Trend Analysis
  • Churned brands with a loyalty user base above 5K are considered.
  • A metric is flagged when it falls 20% or more below the brand's own baseline. Normal month-to-month noise is within ±10%, so 20% is unlikely to be a single bad month, A higher cutoff such as 50% would be near-certain but only fires in the final one or two months — too late to act on.
  • C-store (7) and casual-dining (14) samples are small — read those segments as directional.
  • Without a retained-brand comparison, these charts show what churners had in common, not what distinguishes them from healthy brands.
