The story in brief

Two metrics lead everything else. Repeat-visit rate — the share of new members who return for a second visit — is the earliest signal in the data. It starts sliding seven to eight months before churn and has fallen 20% or more at 78% of brands three months out. New signups follow a month or two behind, flagging about half of brands by M-3. Everything else — active users, redemptions, loyalty sales, signup-to-first-check-in — moves only in the final two to three months, after the decision has usually been made.

Segments behave differently. Casual-dining gives the longest warning: half of these brands showed a falling repeat-visit rate six months out, and it is the only segment where signups collapsed as hard as repeat visits — a sign that servers stopped offering the program at the table. Quick-serve is quiet until three months out, then 84% of brands flag at once; high transaction volume masks decline. Brands under five years in loyalty show trouble earlier and more broadly than mature brands, which hold volume until the last month or two and then drop off a cliff. Pizza is the largest churn cuisine at 20% of brands, mostly after five-plus years. All five c-stores left in 2025 with the strongest engagement in the dataset — a segment decision the loyalty metrics could not see.

If you want six months of warning, watch whether new members come back a second time. If you wait for sales, you get one.

Who churned

Restaurant type × age

Split by restaurant type and years in loyalty

Insights
  • Casual-fast is half of all churn (41 of 83) and skews mature — 59% left after five-plus years.
  • Quick-serve follows the same late pattern (11 of 19 over five years) and carries the largest footprints, so revenue impact per churn is highest here.
  • Casual-dining splits 9/9: an early group where the program never took root and a late group that shrank or was acquired.
  • C-store is the only type dominated by early churn — 4 of 5, all in 2025.
Cuisine × age

Split by cuisine type and years in loyalty

Insights
  • Pizza is the largest churn cuisine at 20% and the most mature — 13 of 17 left after five-plus years.
  • Delivery-heavy pizza brands struggle to hold a first-party loyalty program once third-party apps take over ordering.
  • Early churn concentrates in American, Snack & Ice Cream, Mexican, C-store and Burgers — the same cuisines with the weakest retention baselines.
  • Asian and Ethnic brands are an emerging cluster: 7 of their 11 churns happened in 2026.

How early the warning appears

All brands

% of brands with metric more than 20% below baseline, by month before churn

Insights
  • Repeat-visit rate moves first: a third of brands flagged by M-6, 78% by M-3, 94% by M-1.
  • New signups follow a month or two behind (46% by M-3).
  • Redemptions, active users and loyalty sales all move together in the last 2–3 months — lagging indicators. The drop reflects fewer active members, not weaker offers.
  • Signup-to-first-check-in is the latest signal of all (24% at M-3) — a first check-in is mechanical; the second visit is the first real choice a member makes.
Under 5 years

Brands that churned within 5 years

Insights
  • Younger brands show trouble earlier and more broadly: 71% down on repeat visits by M-4.
  • New signups is a strong early flag here — 32% flagged by M-8, half by M-4.
  • Half of the 'went dark early' brands sit in this group: programs that never embedded.
  • This is where engagement intervention has time to work — 4–5 months of warning.
Over 5 years

Brands that churned after 5+ years

Insights
  • Mature brands hold volume until M-2 and then drop sharply — a renewal-not-taken pattern, not a slow fade.
  • Repeat-visit rate still reaches 78% by M-3, but signups and active users stay flat far longer.
  • Warning time is about one month shorter than for younger brands (3 vs 4 months, statistically significant).
  • Baseline health did not differ by age — tenure bought time, not engagement.
Repeat-visit rate

% of brands >20% below baseline, by restaurant type

Insights
  • Casual-dining gives the longest warning: half flagged by M-6, 78% by M-4.
  • Casual-fast follows the textbook slide — 83% flagged by M-3.
  • Quick-serve is quiet until M-3, then jumps to 84%; high volume masks decline, so QSR gives about a quarter of notice.
  • C-store shows nothing until M-2 — these were commercial exits loyalty KPIs could not have predicted.
New signups

% of brands >20% below baseline, by restaurant type

Insights
  • Casual-dining is the only type where signups collapse as hard as repeat visits — 89% by M-1. Servers stopped offering the program at the table.
  • Casual-fast kept acquiring members (66% at M-1) but could not convert them to habit.
  • Quick-serve signups barely move until M-2.
  • C-store flat at 20% throughout — no acquisition signal at all.

Caveats
  • All churned brands with a loyalty user base above 10K are considered.
  • The 20% below-baseline threshold is a judgment call, chosen to sit above normal month-to-month noise while still firing early.
  • C-store (5) and casual-dining (18) samples are small — read those segments as directional.
  • Without a retained-brand comparison, these charts show what churners had in common, not what distinguishes them from healthy brands.
